ABSTRACT The origin of the X-ray plateau phase in gamma-ray bursts (GRBs) remains an open problem. In particular, it is unclear whether GRBs with different temporal morphologies (i.e. with a rising, flat, or decaying plateau) arise from a common underlying mechanism. Although magnetar energy injection is a leading explanation, previous studies have primarily inferred magnetar properties on a burst-by-burst basis and have not tested the model at the population level. Here we perform the first hierarchical population inference of magnetar parameters for a uniform sample of 185 long GRBs with X-ray plateaus within a conditional Poisson point-process framework. It is found that the observed plateau population is well reproduced by physically plausible magnetar populations. The inferred parameter distributions show no strong statistical separation among subclasses with different plateau morphologies. Nevertheless, all subclasses show a substantial intrinsic luminosity scatter, σL, int ∼ 0.5– 1.0 dex, whereas the intrinsic duration scatter remains considerably smaller. The results provide a population-level test of the magnetar interpretation of GRB X-ray plateaus, showing that the observed diversity of plateau morphologies does not require distinct magnetar populations.

1. INTRODUCTION The X-ray plateau is one of the most intriguing features observed in the early afterglows of gamma-ray bursts (GRBs) (Gehrels et al. 2006; Granot & Kumar 2006; Zhang et al. 2006). Its temporal behavior spans a wide range, including rising, nearly flat, and shallow-decaying plateaus (Tang et al. 2019; Deng et al. 2023; Ronchini et al. 2023; Du et al. 2024). Analyses of X-ray afterglows, prompt-emission properties, and population statistics have consistently shown that GRBs exhibiting different plateau morphologies share broadly similar observational characteristics (Srinivasaragavan et al. 2020; Du et al. 2024; Dong et al. 2026). Although numerous models have been proposed to explain these behaviors (Fan & Piran 2006; Liang et al. 2007; Dainotti et al. 2008; Yamazaki 2009), it remains unclear whether the observed diversity reflects intrinsically distinct populations or a common mechanism operating under different conditions (Bhardwaj et al. 2023; Guglielmi et al. 2024; Lenart et al. 2025). Among the proposed explanations for GRB X-ray plateaus, energy injection from a newborn millisecond magnetar is one of the leading scenarios (Dai & Lu 1998a,b; Zhang & M´ esz´ aros 2001; Fan & Piran 2006). In the standard dipole spin-down model, the plateau luminosity and characteristic timescale are primarily controlled by the surface magnetic field strength Bp and initial spin period P0, allowing plateau observations to probe the properties of the central engine (Metzger et al. 2011; Gompertz et al. 2013; Hou et al. 2021; Tian et al. 2024). Previous studies of GRB X-ray plateaus within the magnetar framework have largely relied on event-by-event analyses, either through analytic inversion of the dipole spin-down relations (Yu et al. 2010; Rowlinson et al. 2014; L¨ u & Zhang 2014) or direct fitting of plateau light curves (Stratta et al. 2018; Deng et al. 2026). Early applications used a small number of X-ray plateaus to constrain the magnetar spin period and magnetic field strength (Lyons et al. 2010), and were subsequently extended to short GRBs and extended-emission bursts (Rowlinson et al. 2013; Gompertz et al. 2014; L¨ u et al. 2015). More recent studies have applied these methods to larger GRB samples to investigate the distributions and correlations of the inferred magnetar parameters (Lan et al. 2025; Zhou et al. 2026; Li et al. 2026). Despite extensive studies of individual GRBs, the intrinsic population distribution of magnetar parameters remains poorly constrained. Existing population-level results are generally constructed from collections of individually inferred parameters, while selection effects are not incorporated directly into the inference framework (Thrane & Talbot 2019; Mandel et al. 2019; Loredo & Hendry 2019). In this work, we model the plateau sample directly in the latent magnetar-parameter space and account for selection effects self-consistently, enabling a direct test of whether different plateau morphologies require distinct underlying population. The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 describes the hierarchical Bayesian framework used to infer the population distribution of magnetar parameters. Section 3 presents our results for the total plateau sample, the morphology-based subsamples, and their comparison. Section 4 summarizes the main conclusions. Section 5 discusses the implications of our findings.

METHODS A Bayesian point-process framework was developed by Loredo & Wasserman (1995, 1998a,b) for GRB population studies, where the observed events are modeled as a realization of an underlying source population filtered through the detector response. Measurement uncertainties and selection effects are incorporated directly into the likelihood, enabling inference on the intrinsic population distribution rather than the detected sample alone. Such hierarchical Bayesian and point-process methods are now widely used in transient-population studies. In the GRB context, they have been used to infer luminosity and prompt-emission distributions (Shahmoradi & Nemiroff 2015), delay-time distributions (Farah et al. 2020), cosmic rate evolution (Salafia et al. 2023), and jet or luminosity structures (Biscoveanu et al. 2020; Pracchia & Sharan Salafia 2026). Similar approaches are also widely used in gravitational-wave population inference (Farr et al. 2015; Barrett et al. 2018; Mandel et al. 2019), and have been applied to fast radio burst (Wang et al. 2026) and exoplanet population studies (Foreman-Mackey et al. 2014). Here, we adopt a conditional Poisson point-process (CPP) likelihood, which conditions on the observed sample size and therefore focuses on the shape of the underlying population rather than its absolute normalization. Selection effects are incorporated through the detectable fraction, allowing intrinsic population properties to be inferred from an incomplete observed sample. The CPP likelihood is equivalent to the full point-process likelihood after marginalizing over the global event-rate normalization under the prior π(R) ∝ 1/R (Fishbach et al. 2018; Mandel et al. 2019; Loredo & Hendry 2019). RESULTS Our analysis uses the uniform sample of 185 long GRBs with measured redshifts and well-defined X-ray plateaus compiled by Dong et al. (2026). Following that work, the sample is divided into rising ( −0.5 < α 1 ≤ − 0.1, 16 GRBs), flat ( −0.1 < α 1 ≤ 0.1, 75 GRBs), and decaying (0 .1 < α 1 ≤ 0.5, 94 GRBs) subclasses. Previous analyses found these groups to have statistically consistent population properties despite their different plateau morphologies. We adopt the K-corrected plateau luminosity LX, observed break time Ta, obs , and redshift reported by Dong et al. (2026) as inputs to the CPP analysis. The minimum observed plateau flux is used as an empirical flux scale, log 10 (Fth /1 erg cm −2 s−1) = −12 .76. For the Swift plateau bursts, we adopt log 10 (tmin /1 s) = 1 and log 10 (tmax /1 s) = 6, consistent with the observed range of plateau measurements (Li et al. 2012; Tang et al. 2019). 3.1. 3.2. CPP Analysis of the Rising, Flat, and Decaying Subsamples 

To investigate whether different plateau morphologies require distinct magnetar populations, we apply the CPP framework separately to the rising, flat, and decaying subsamples. The resulting parameter constraints are summarized in Table 1, and the corresponding posterior distributions are shown in Figure A2. The inferred magnetar properties are broadly consistent among the three subclasses. The characteristic magnetic fields cluster around log 10 Bc ∼ 15 .5, while the characteristic spin periods lie within the range log 10 Pc ∼ 0.7 − 0.9. Figure 3 reconstructs the posterior distributions in the log 10 Bc −log 10 Pc plane. The high-probability regions exhibit substantial overlap, and no obvious abnormal distribution is evident among the three subclasses. Among the three subclasses, the rising sample exhibits the broadest posterior distribution. This is probably due to its substantially smaller sample size ( N = 16), which provides weaker statistical constraints than the flat and decaying samples. Nevertheless, its posterior region remains similar with those inferred from the other two subclasses. 3.3. Comparison Among All the Plateau Samples

The violin widths represent posterior probability densities, while the white markers and error bars denote the median values and corresponding 68% credible intervals. 4. CONCLUSION In this study, we applied a hierarchical Bayesian framework based on a conditional Poisson point-process likelihood to infer the population properties of magnetar central engines in GRB X-ray plateaus. The method constrains the underlying magnetar population within the adopted forward model while accounting for observational selection effects and measurement uncertainties. Applying the framework to a uniform sample of 185 Swift /XRT plateau GRBs, we found that the posterior distributions satisfactorily reproduce the observed distributions of plateau luminosity, break time, and redshift. The posterior distributions favor a physically plausible population of highly magnetized millisecond magnetars, with a characteristic spin period of ∼ 6.8 ms and a magnetic field strength of ∼ 3.3 × 10 15 G. Applying the same framework to the rising, flat, and decaying plateau subsamples, we find no strong statistical evidence for distinct magnetar populations. The inferred parameter distributions exhibit substantial overlap and remain broadly consistent with those obtained for the full sample. Despite this overall similarity, all subclasses require a relatively large intrinsic luminosity scatter, σL, int ∼ 0.5–1.0 dex, whereas the intrinsic duration scatter is considerably smaller. These results suggest that different plateau morphologies can be understood within a common magnetar framework, although additional sources of luminosity diversity may still be present within the current model.

Implications for Plateau Diversity 

Our CPP analysis indicates that the rising, flat, and decaying plateau samples are compatible with a common underlying magnetar population. The observed diversity of plateau morphologies therefore need not imply distinct central-engine populations. It may instead reflect differences in the efficiency or environment through which energy from a similar engine powers the afterglow. In external-shock models, a broad range of plateau morphologies can arise naturally from variations in outflow properties (Dereli-B´ egu´ e et al. 2022), jet structure (Beniamini et al. 2020; Oganesyan et al. 2020), and the circum-burst environment (Srinivasaragavan et al. 2020; Lan et al. 2025). Observational effects may further contribute to the apparent diversity. Yamazaki (2009) showed that some plateau features can depend on the choice of reference time, while recent large-sample studies found that the XRT observation start time strongly influences plateau de-tectability and light-curve complexity (Guidorzi et al. 2025; Vigliano et al. 2026). These results suggest that several morphology-dependent correlations can be weakened or even removed by observational effects, and therefore should not be interpreted solely as evidence for intrinsic differences in the central engine. Pure magnetic-dipole spin-down predicts an energy-injection rate that is initially nearly constant and then declines, so it cannot by itself produce a pronounced rising plateau. This is a limitation of the simplest spin-down prescription rather than of the magnetar framework as a whole.
