# Spatio-temporal coarse-grained Hawkes processes 

## Tomoya Uda ∗

## , Shinsuke Koyama †

## October 2, 2026 

Abstract 

Spatio-temporal Hawkes processes are widely used to model self-exciting event data, but conventional inference methods require access to the oc-currence time and location of every event. In many applications, however, observations are available only as counts aggregated over temporal inter-vals and spatial regions. We propose a spatio-temporal coarse-grained Hawkes process, a discrete-time count model that approximates aggre-gated observations generated by an underlying spatio-temporal Hawkes process. The proposed model constructs effective excitation kernels by averaging the temporal and spatial triggering effects over spatio-temporal bins while explicitly incorporating excitation occurring within the same temporal bin. This formulation enables direct modeling of aggregated count data without introducing latent event times or locations. We char-acterize the first- and second-order properties of the proposed process and derive asymptotic approximation errors for the stationary mean and autocovariance relative to those of the corresponding aggregated Hawkes process in the joint limit of fine temporal and spatial discretization. The analysis shows that the proposed model provides a higher-order approxi-mation than the conventional binned Poisson approximation by account-ing for intra-bin excitation. Numerical experiments confirm the theoret-ical results and demonstrate accurate approximation over a broad range of aggregation scales. We further develop a moment-based estimation procedure and apply the proposed framework to earthquake occurrence data through an aggregated Epidemic-Type Aftershock Sequence (ETAS) model. The results indicate that the proposed approach provides a com-putationally efficient alternative for inference and prediction from spatio-temporally aggregated event data. 

# 1 Introduction 

Many phenomena in nature and society exhibit dependence across both space and time. In a wide range of applications, the occurrence of an event increases the likelihood of subsequent events in its temporal and spatial neighborhood.

1arXiv:2610.01151v1 [stat.ME] 1 Oct 2026 The Hawkes process [1, 2] provides a natural point-process framework for de-scribing such self-exciting dynamics. Spatio-temporal Hawkes processes have been extensively used to characterize event patterns in seismology [3, 4, 5], criminology [6, 7], and traffic incident analysis [8], while temporal Hawkes pro-cesses have found applications in neurophysiology [9, 10], finance [11, 12, 13], and social media analytics [14, 15, 16]. A fundamental requirement of the Hawkes process is access to the occur-rence time and location of each event. In many applications, however, such fine-grained information is unavailable or impractical to process. Instead, ob-servations are often provided as counts aggregated over temporal intervals and spatial regions: for example, the number of earthquakes recorded in a geographic area during a given period. Such aggregation induces a loss of information about the precise configurations of events, including the temporal and spatial relation-ships that generate self-excitation. Developing statistical models that preserve the essential dependence structure of the underlying Hawkes process while op-erating directly on aggregated counts is therefore an important methodological problem. For temporal aggregation, the process obtained by counting events from a continuous-time Hawkes process within fixed time intervals is referred to as a binned Hawkes process. Since the binned process is generated by first real-izing the continuous-time events and then aggregating them, it is not itself a standard discrete-time Hawkes process, and direct likelihood-based inference is generally unavailable. Existing approaches can broadly be divided into two classes. The first treats the unobserved event configurations within each bin as latent variables. Examples include Monte Carlo expectation-maximization (MC-EM) methods [17, 18], Pseudo-Marginal Metropolis-Hastings (PMMH) [19], and Bayesian inference methods [20]. These approaches can retain detailed information about the underlying Hawkes dynamics, but repeated sampling or imputation of latent events can be computationally expensive, particularly for large data sets. The second class constructs a discrete-time model directly for the observed counts. Examples include the binned Poisson approximation and integer-valued autoregressive (INAR) models [17, 21]. Such approaches offer substantial com-putational advantages because their likelihoods or objective functions have rel-atively simple forms. However, standard discretization approximations can fail to capture intra-bin excitation, whereby an event occurring within a bin trig-gers additional events during the same bin. This effect can be substantial when the aggregation interval is not sufficiently short. The temporal coarse-grained Hawkes process proposed in [22] addresses this limitation by constructing an ef-fective discrete-time excitation kernel that incorporates intra-bin excitation. In particular, it was shown to provide a higher-order approximation to the binned Hawkes process in terms of its spectral density than the binned Poisson approx-imation. The temporal aggregation problem also has a direct computational inter-pretation. For a continuous-time Hawkes process with a general excitation kernel, naive likelihood evaluation can require O(N 2) operations, where N is 2the number of observed events. Aggregating events into bins can substantially reduce this computational burden, and discretized inference methods have there-fore been developed even in settings where the exact event times are available [23, 24]. Similar considerations become more pronounced for spatio-temporal processes, for which the number of events can be large. Spatial aggregation can consequently provide an additional reduction in data complexity while yielding observations that are naturally represented as counts over spatial regions. Despite these advantages, extending coarse-grained modeling from temporal to spatio-temporal aggregation is nontrivial. Temporal and spatial aggregation jointly obscure the event configurations that determine excitation, and the re-sulting approximation must account for excitation both across and within the spatio-temporal bins. In particular, a model obtained by independently dis-cretizing the temporal and spatial coordinates need not preserve the statistical structure of the aggregated process. A useful coarse-grained model should in-stead provide an effective excitation mechanism that represents the average in-fluence of unobserved events over both dimensions while retaining the essential second-order dependence induced by self-excitation. In this paper, we develop a spatio-temporal coarse-grained Hawkes process as an extension of the temporal coarse-grained Hawkes process of [22]. The proposed model describes event counts aggregated over both temporal intervals and spatial regions and constructs an effective excitation kernel by averaging the underlying spatio-temporal excitation effects over the corresponding bins. A key feature is that the construction retains excitation between events occurring within the same temporal bin, thereby incorporating intra-bin effects that are absent from conventional discretization approaches. Because the exact locations of individual events may be unavailable, the spatial averaging can be performed with respect to an assumed distribution of event locations within each spatial region. This allows the model to exploit spatial excitation without requiring reconstruction of the latent event locations. We establish the statistical properties of the proposed model and its rela-tionship to the corresponding aggregated process generated by an underlying continuous-time spatio-temporal Hawkes process. In particular, we derive the stationary mean and autocovariance and characterize the orders of their ap-proximation errors. These results provide a theoretical basis for evaluating the accuracy of the proposed coarse-graining procedure as a function of the temporal and spatial aggregation scales. We further compare the resulting approxima-tion with the binned Poisson approximation, thereby quantifying the benefit of explicitly incorporating within-bin excitation. Numerical experiments examine the dependence of the approximation error on the temporal and spatial bin sizes. Finally, we apply the proposed framework to earthquake data and demonstrate parameter estimation from aggregated spatio-temporal event counts. The contribution of this work is therefore twofold. First, we extend the coarse-grained Hawkes framework from temporal aggregation to the substan-tially more general setting of joint spatio-temporal aggregation. Second, we provide a theoretical characterization of the approximation induced by this coarse-graining and demonstrate its practical utility for inference from spatially 3and temporally aggregated event data. The proposed framework offers a com-putationally tractable alternative to latent-event inference while retaining a key feature of Hawkes dynamics: self-excitation within the aggregation scale. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews spatio-temporal Hawkes processes. Section 3.1 introduces the spatio-temporal coarse-grained Hawkes process and derives its statistical properties. The theoretical approximation results and numerical evaluations are presented in Sections 3.2 and 3.3, respectively. Section 4 describes the parameter estimation procedure and its application to earthquake data.

This study introduced a class of discrete-time stochastic processes for spatio-temporally aggregated event-count data that retain the self-exciting behavior of continuous-time Hawkes processes. We defined this class by coarse-graining continuous-time Hawkes processes and requiring the resulting residual process to satisfy the first- and second-order conditional moment properties inherited from the martingale increments. We evaluated the approximation errors of the stationary mean and autoco-variance relative to those of the corresponding binned Hawkes process in the joint limit ∆ t → 0 and |S| → 0. The resulting asymptotic analysis shows that the proposed model has smaller discretization errors than the binned Poisson approximation. Numerical experiments further demonstrate that these errors can be one to two orders of magnitude smaller than those of the binned Poisson approximation, even for relatively coarse temporal and spatial discretizations. We also applied the proposed framework to earthquake occurrence data mod-eled by the ETAS model, a continuous-time spatio-temporal Hawkes process. Although our method did not match the ETAS model in test log-likelihood, which is expected because parameter estimation was based only on aggregated occurrence data, it achieved predictive performance comparable to that of ex-isting neural point-process models. At the same time, the proposed method required substantially less computational time in the experiments. These re-sults suggest that the proposed framework can provide a practical alternative when event-level temporal and spatial information is unavailable or too costly to process. The proposed framework has advantages over the binned Poisson approxi-mation from both temporal and spatial perspectives. Temporally, it explicitly incorporates excitation among events occurring within the same temporal bin through the intra-bin excitation coefficient ϕ0. This effect is particularly impor-tant for temporal kernels with substantial mass near zero, such as exponential kernels, for which an event can strongly influence subsequent events within the same temporal bin. Spatially, the framework represents event locations within each spatial bin through a prescribed distribution. This allows available spatial information to be incorporated into the coarse-grained model and accommo-dates spatial bins with different sizes and shapes. Moreover, because the spatial excitation kernel is defined through an integral over each bin, the total spa-tial excitation is preserved under the normalization conditions imposed on the kernel. The numerical experiments indicate that these features make the pro-posed model relatively robust to coarse spatio-temporal aggregation, which is particularly useful when fine-scale event data are unavailable. From a computational perspective, the proposed method provides a direct way to control computational cost through the temporal and spatial resolu-tions. A single evaluation of the loss function has computational complexity 

O  n2d + nd 2 + d3, where n and d denote the numbers of temporal and spatial bins, respectively. Importantly, this complexity depends on the number of bins rather than on the total number of events. Consequently, aggregation can sub-23 stantially reduce the computational burden when the original event catalog is large. This study has several limitations. First, the proposed process is defined only through its first- and second-order conditional moment properties rather than through a complete probability law. Consequently, likelihood-based in-ference and direct simulation of the process require additional distributional assumptions. Second, the theoretical properties of the proposed parameter esti-mator remain to be established. In particular, future work should investigate the consistency and asymptotic distribution of the estimator, as well as its dynam-ical and dependence properties. It would also be valuable to develop explicit distributional specifications for the coarse-grained process and to extend the framework to nonlinear and nonstationary Hawkes processes and to other forms of aggregated event data.
